Quality & governance

You decide what it can do.

Corporate guardrails turn your policies and responsibilities into practical limits for AI: approved information, permitted actions, review requirements and escalation paths. We agree and test these around each role so useful work can move while your people retain authority.

Customer ownership & access

Your knowledge.
Your decisions. Your direction.

You should be able to understand the knowledge behind the work, question it and keep the material that belongs to your business.

Your work stays yours.

You retain your business knowledge, outputs and customer-specific workflows and skills—including the tailored improvements created during the service. Agreed export formats let you keep and use that work.

Customer knowledge you can review.

Your managed service includes agreed access to review knowledge, contribute corrections and export customer material. Permissions determine direct edits, approvals, deletion and retention arrangements.

Clear rights to the software.

A managed subscription pays for operation, maintained connections and agreed improvement. A separately purchased perpetual licence lets your team run the delivered software on supported infrastructure. FUSED retains its software IP under either model; your customer-specific work remains yours.

Agree the handover before you need it.

Agree export formats, documentation, access removal and any transition support before launch. If a managed subscription ends, you keep your customer material and the managed service stops. A perpetual licence and any migration are separately quoted, rather than automatically included in the subscription. Providers, permissions and retention arrangements are documented. A decision matters when it changes how the business works. Your colleague can help capture the decision, identify affected people and guidance, and flag repeated exceptions. Where included in scope, document and workflow skills can carry approved changes into procedures and everyday work, with a record of what happened.

A quality workflow in practice

From reported issue to reviewed closure.

The colleague can organise the process and keep it moving. The people responsible for quality still judge causes, risks and closure.

01 / CAPTURE

Record the issue

Structure what happened, when, where and what initial evidence exists.

02 / ORGANISE

Assemble evidence

Link relevant records, documents, observations and previous events.

03 / INVESTIGATE

Support the analysis

Prepare timelines, patterns and questions while a responsible person judges cause.

04 / ACT

Coordinate actions

Maintain owners, dates, dependencies, reminders and supporting evidence.

05 / REVIEW

Confirm and improve

Present closure evidence, recurring patterns and proposed system improvements.

Trust earned through actual work

Trust is earned.
One responsibility at a time.

We agree what good work looks like and test ordinary requests, missing information and awkward cases. The checks reflect the consequences of getting that particular job wrong.

BEFORE MORE RESPONSIBILITY

Check the work.

Check accuracy, source use, corrections and handovers across representative work. A convincing answer or time spent in training is not enough on its own.

WHEN YOU AGREE

Agree what it may do.

Record the actions, sources, limits and decisions that still need a person. New skills and broader responsibilities require their own checks and approval.

AS THE WORK CONTINUES

Keep checking as things change.

Monitor results. If quality drops or something changes, pause affected actions or return them to review. Check the fix before agreeing to resume.

Learning more does not grant permission to do more. Updates to business knowledge do not change a skill’s authority. A skill can remain useful with a person reviewing every output; the review arrangements are agreed for your service.

Corporate guardrails in practice

Your rules, built into the work.

The controls belong in the workflow and the connected systems as well as the instructions. We agree access permissions, approval points and records, then test how the role behaves when a request crosses a boundary.

01 / DEFINE

Set access and authority

Define the sources a colleague may read, the tools it may use and the actions that need approval. Public website conversations use an approved information set, with internal records kept outside that access.

  • Purpose and expected results
  • Approved information
  • Permissions
  • Decision owners
02 / WATCH

Check, record and escalate

Check outputs against the agreed task and sources. Record significant actions and route missing information, conflicting instructions or sensitive decisions to a person.

  • Activity records
  • Unusual situations
  • Failed handovers
  • Review measures
03 / IMPROVE

Change with approval

Review failures and recurring exceptions. Agree changes to instructions or permissions, retest affected work and document who approved the change. More knowledge does not automatically mean broader authority.

  • Cause analysis
  • Corrective actions
  • Change approval
  • Check that the fix worked
