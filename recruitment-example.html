Applied work · Talent screening & shortlisting

Find the right fit—not merely the best-looking CV.

A recruitment workflow designed to help leadership manage high applicant volume, compare evidence consistently and discover the questions that matter before making a human appointment.

Context
Growing operating business
Function
Hiring & talent
Constraint
High volume and weak fit signals
Response
Structured screening workflow
Applied experienceAn example from the founders’ recruitment work in an operating business. Business and applicant details are withheld. This is their experience, not a FUSED client case study.

The management problem

The team needed a clearer way to compare applicants.

When a role attracts a large and inconsistent pile of applications, leadership cannot give every CV the same depth of attention. Relevant experience can be hard to spot, and inconsistent notes make the applications harder to compare.

The risk is not just missing a qualification. It is discovering too late that the person, role, team and manager do not fit one another.

THE DESIGN INSIGHT
The right person is not a universal answer. It is the right person for this role, in this business, under this manager.

Start with the actual job and operating context, then use AI to prepare better evidence and better questions—not to make the appointment.

What changed

Bring the evidence and the gaps into view.

The workflow made each application easier to compare without pretending a score could replace leadership judgement.

01

Define the actual role

Clarify must-haves, deal-breakers, working conditions, manager expectations, team dynamics and success criteria.

02

Structure the evidence

Organise CVs and supporting material so experience, claims, gaps and relevant examples can be compared consistently.

03

Identify what needs testing

Identify missing evidence and questions that could confirm or challenge the picture on paper.

04

Prepare the hiring review

Present shortlist reasoning and tailored questions so leadership can spend time on the candidates most worth understanding.

The CV is a starting point

Paper strength is not the same as operating fit.

A CV rarely explains how someone responds under pressure, learns unfamiliar work, relates to a particular manager or strengthens a specific team. The workflow used the application to identify what still needed to be tested.

BEFORE

Screening relied on volume and first impressions.

  • Applications were difficult to review fairly
  • Good candidates could disappear in the pile
  • Paper strength was mistaken for role fit
  • Concerns and training needs surfaced late
  • Shortlist reasoning varied by reviewer
AFTER

The team received comparable summaries and useful questions.

  • Evidence was compared with defined requirements
  • Gaps and claims needing clarification were visible
  • Screening questions were tailored to each candidate
  • Manager, team and training fit entered earlier
  • Decision time moved towards stronger candidates

What remained human

The goal was to protect judgement—not replace it.

People approved progression and rejection, interpreted context, conducted interviews, assessed the relationship and made the appointment. The system reduced repetitive preparation so that human attention could be used where it mattered.

What this demonstrates

Better preparation made room for better conversations.

The useful work sat around the appointment: defining the real role, organising evidence, finding the gaps and preparing better questions. The business still decided who could succeed there.

The lesson for FUSED is to define the actual job first, then use technology to give the responsible people better information to work with.
